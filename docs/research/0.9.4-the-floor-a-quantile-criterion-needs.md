# The floor a quantile criterion needs

A design, written before any code, for one change: the floor a comparison requires before it will call a
direction on a quantile. Every number below was produced by running this product against a real repository
through the real command line, or read from source with its `file:line`.

## What is wrong, measured twice on an unchanged system

`compareMetric` refuses a direction below three samples per side, and applies that one number to every
metric including a quantile (`packages/comparison/src/compare.ts`, `minimumSamplesPerSide`). A p95 of three
runs is the slowest of the three.

Measured end to end on `openai-cs-agents-demo` at `bd7bfca`, driven keyless through the SDK's own
`ModelProvider`, with a `metric_improvement` criterion on `durationMs.p95` at a 15 per cent threshold:

    samples per side   p95 change on a system nobody changed   criterion
    3                  -15.3 per cent                          satisfied
    3 (second attempt) -23.4 per cent                          satisfied
    20                  -5.1 per cent                          would not be satisfied

The 3-sample runs banked a performance win on a change that was never made. In the same comparison the
**mean** of those same three runs was reported `indeterminate`, "difference is only 1.2 times the combined
standard error", so the engine said in one line that the samples supported no claim about duration and in
the next that duration had improved by fifteen per cent.

At twenty samples the tail estimate is as stable as the mean: p95 moved -5.1 per cent where the mean moved
-4.5 per cent, both well inside any threshold a rule states. **The floor is what separates the two rows,
and twenty is reachable: those twenty repetitions took about thirty five seconds.**

## The number is already decided, in this repository, at schema level

    export const QUANTILE_MIN_SAMPLES = { p50: 3, p90: 10, p95: 20, p99: 100 } as const;

`packages/schema/src/benchmark.ts:33-37`, whose own comment says that below the threshold a quantile is
"reported as unavailable rather than computed from too few points". `summarize` in
`packages/domain/src/statistics.ts:39-53` honours it and records what it withheld, which is why a scenario
result prints

    p95 withheld: it needs at least 20 samples

while the comparison of the same runs computes a p95 from three and calls a direction on it. **One
repository, one question, two answers, and the goal criteria read the weaker one.**

So this design invents no threshold. It makes the comparison honour the contract the repository already
wrote.

## Decision

> **A quantile's direction requires the samples that quantile needs, per `QUANTILE_MIN_SAMPLES`, on both
> sides. A criterion naming a quantile is issued only where the plan can produce them.**

Three places, one question.

1. **`compareMetric`** takes the floor for the named quantile rather than one number for every metric, and
   the refusal names the real requirement: "needs at least 20 samples per side, has 3 and 3".
2. **Goal creation** asks the same question of the metric a criterion names, so a `metric_improvement` on
   `durationMs.p95` is issued only where both sides will carry twenty. This is the existing
   `metricIsDecidable` reading the metric's own requirement instead of one shared floor.
3. **The document says which requirement failed**, naming the metric and its number, because "the baseline
   supplies 3 samples" means something different for a p50 than for a p95.

**What this does not change, and deliberately.** A quantile stays gated on a sample floor rather than on
`differenceIsMeaningful`. That decision is settled and is right: the mean test compares means, and a tail
that moved while the mean held still is the case a p95 criterion exists for. What was wrong was the size of
the floor, not the choice to use one. The caveat that travels on the delta stays, because twenty samples
make an order statistic stable rather than make it a spread test.

## What it costs, stated rather than discovered

Of the three rules that can carry a `metric_improvement`
(`packages/goals/src/create.ts`, `RELATIVE_IMPROVEMENT_BY_RULE`):

| rule | metric | floor after | affected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `agent-count-does-not-pay-for-itself` | `durationMs.p50` | 3 | no |
| `workers-receive-comparably-large-context` | `inputTokens` | 3, the mean test | no |
| `independent-calls-run-sequentially` | `durationMs.p95` | **20** | **yes** |

So one rule of three is affected, and it is the one whose criterion was measured banking a false pass.

**A goal cut from that rule at the default three repetitions will state no performance criterion.** It will
say the baseline supplies three samples where a p95 needs twenty, and an operator who records twenty and
cuts the goal again gets the criterion. That is the same shape as every other omission this loop already
makes, and it is the honest one: a criterion that can be satisfied by noise is worse than a criterion that
is absent, because the first says done.

**The plan does not raise its own repetition count to compensate.** It could print `--repetitions 20` and
that would fix only the candidate side: the baseline is a recording and cannot be re-run into existence, so
a plan that raised the candidate alone would still name a command that cannot decide the term, which is the
failure `create.ts` argues against at length. Naming the requirement and leaving the operator to record it
is the honest form.

## Falsifiers

- **F1. A quantile direction is refused below the quantile's own floor.** `durationMs.p95` from ten samples
  a side is `indeterminate` naming twenty. **Old tree: `improved`**, because ten clears a floor of three.
- **F2. The criterion is not issued where its metric cannot be decided.** A goal from
  `independent-calls-run-sequentially` with a three sample baseline states no `metric_improvement`. **Old
  tree: states it.**
- **F3. The criterion is issued where it can be decided.** The same goal with a twenty sample baseline and
  twenty prescribed repetitions states it. A **guard** that this change omits by requirement rather than
  always.
- **F4. A p50 is unaffected.** `durationMs.p50` from three samples a side still decides. A **guard**, and
  the one that would catch this change being applied as one raised number for every quantile.
- **F5. The mean is unaffected.** `durationMs` from three samples a side still goes through
  `differenceIsMeaningful`. A **guard**.

Two existing tests move and keep their meaning. The test that proves a tail sees what a mean hides uses ten
samples and will use twenty; the test that proves a quantile is refused below the floor asserts three and
will assert twenty.

## What the measurement said, after the change

Driven through the real command line on the same repository and the same unchanged system.

    baseline samples   prescribed reruns   criterion
    20                 3                   withheld, and the plan says why
    20                 20                  stated, decided, and not satisfied

The withheld case reports, in the goal document itself:

    no criterion is stated on durationMs.p95, which needs 20 samples on each side:
    this plan supplies 20 samples from the recorded baseline and 3 repetitions from the rerun

The stated case decides it and refuses it:

    durationMs.p95 changed by -5.9 percent against a required 15 percent (samples 20 and 20)
    (compared as order statistics of the runs on each side, without a spread test)

Against `-15.3 per cent` and `-23.4 per cent` satisfied at three samples on the same unchanged system, this
is the whole of what the change was for: the criterion is still decidable, and noise no longer satisfies it.

## Corpus impact

None expected. Nothing here touches a discovery reader, an adapter, a matcher or a rule, and the corpus
expectations compare no goal, no comparison and no criterion.
