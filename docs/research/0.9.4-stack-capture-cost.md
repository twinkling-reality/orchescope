# What a bounded stack capture costs

Measurement 1 of the four [the source attribution scope](0.9.4-source-attribution-at-runtime.md) names, run
before any shim change. It is the question that decides whether the change is possible at all, because the
shim's own rule is that instrumentation which can change the behaviour of the thing it measures is not
instrumentation.

**Answer: affordable on both runtimes, and the bound genuinely bounds.** A capture that reaches five frames
costs 3.3 microseconds on Node and 0.5 on Python, which is 2.35 per cent and 0.28 per cent of one loopback
HTTP request. Two constraints came out of the measurement rather than out of the design, and both are
recorded below.

Measured on `node v24.15.0` and `python 3.14.6`, on this machine. Scripts under
`scratchpad/capture/`. Every figure is a median of medians: an inner loop of 200
captures timed as one sample, 200 to 400 samples, after a warmup of 20,000.

## The control that matters most: cost tracks the bound, not the stack

If a capture walked the whole stack regardless of the bound, a deep target would pay for its own depth and
no ceiling would help. It does not.

**Node, nanoseconds per capture, set and restore included, reading the file and line of every frame:**

| stack depth | limit 1 | limit 3 | limit 5 | limit 10 | limit 20 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 1608 | 2497 | 3256 | 4773 | 5797 |
| 20 | 1538 | 2416 | 3235 | 5283 | 9225 |
| 50 | 1589 | 2421 | 3264 | 5251 | 9187 |
| 100 | 1595 | 2452 | 3238 | 5220 | 9224 |
| 200 | 1650 | 2523 | 3309 | 5169 | 9376 |

**Python, nanoseconds per capture, walking `f_back` and reading `f_code.co_filename` and `f_lineno`:**

| stack depth | limit 1 | limit 3 | limit 5 | limit 10 | limit 20 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 138 | 316 | 478 | 948 | 1158 |
| 20 | 138 | 309 | 486 | 893 | 1729 |
| 50 | 135 | 311 | 476 | 926 | 1792 |

| 200 | 135 | 310 | 486 | 899 | 1730 |

**Flat across a fortyfold change in depth, on both runtimes.** The cost is a function of the ceiling and of
nothing else. A prohibition on unbounded work is therefore satisfiable here rather than merely intended.

## The denominator

A figure in microseconds means nothing until it sits beside the thing it is added to. The hardest possible
denominator is a loopback request, which has no network in it at all; a request to a real provider is three
orders of magnitude slower and makes every ratio below smaller.

**Node, nanoseconds per outbound POST to `127.0.0.1`:**

| case | p50 | p90 | p99 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| bare `fetch` | 117,125 | 162,083 | 405,792 |
| through `instrumentedFetch` | 138,542 | 200,084 | 642,916 |

**The shim already costs 21,417 nanoseconds per request, 18.3 per cent of a bare loopback request.** That is
the number a capture has to be judged against, because it is what this repository has already decided is an
acceptable price for evidence.

**Python, loopback POST through `urllib`: p50 170,917, p90 193,667, p99 307,000.**

| | Node | Python |
| --- | --- | --- |
| capture at limit 5 | 3,250 ns, **2.35 %** of one instrumented request | 486 ns, **0.28 %** of one request |
| capture at limit 5, against what the shim already adds | **15 %** of it | not comparable, no Python shim exists |

## A generous bound is also affordable, measured rather than extrapolated

The open question this measurement cannot answer is how many frames it takes to get past an SDK to the
repository's own file. So the useful thing is to price a bound large enough that the question can be settled
on usefulness rather than on cost.

| | Node, depth 50 | Node, depth 200 | Python, depth 50 | Python, depth 200 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| limit 5 | 3,223 | 3,223 | 431 | 438 |
| limit 20 | 9,751 | 9,256 | 1,557 | 1,509 |
| limit 50 | 21,280 | 22,002 | 3,737 | 3,743 |
| limit 100 | 24,167 | 41,222 | 4,298 | 7,691 |

**A fifty frame bound costs about what the shim already costs**, 22 microseconds against 21, which is 16 per
cent of a loopback request and a hundredth of a per cent of a real provider call. Measurement 2 can pick the
bound on whether it reaches a repository frame, and treat cost as settled up to about fifty.

## Memory

`node --expose-gc`, 200,000 captures per limit, heap sampled before, after, and after a forced collection.

| limit | bytes in flight per capture | bytes still held after collection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 28.5 | 0.078 |
| 5 | 27.8 | 0.029 |
| 10 | 27.8 | 0.001 |
| 20 | 24.7 | 0 |

Twenty eight bytes, transient, independent of the bound, and nothing retained. **This holds only because the
rule reads the file and the line and drops the frames.** A V8 `CallSite` can hold the receiver and the
function of the frame it describes, so keeping one keeps whatever that closure closed over, and a shim that
stored frames would be a queue with no ceiling wearing a different name.

## What a run actually costs

The eight traced corpus entries record 4, 4, 5, 6, 6, 6, 16, 26 and 31 spans per run. At the busiest of
them, a bounded capture at limit 5 costs **101 microseconds on Node and 15 on Python, for the whole run.**

The rate-independent form of the same argument is stronger and should be the one quoted: a capture happens
once per outbound HTTP request, and an outbound HTTP request cannot be faster than a loopback one. So the
capture can never exceed 2.35 per cent of the fastest request that can exist, whatever the rate.

## Two constraints the measurement produced

**Never `inspect.stack()`, and the number is the argument.** It scales with depth and it is not close:
106,777 nanoseconds at depth 5, 428,358 at depth 50, **1,524,508 at depth 200, which is 892 per cent of a
whole loopback request.** `traceback.extract_stack` is bounded but formats every frame and costs sixteen
times the `f_back` walk, 7,792 nanoseconds at limit 5. The `f_back` walk is the only affordable form.

**Both V8 globals must be saved and restored, synchronously.** `Error.stackTraceLimit` and
`Error.prepareStackTrace` are process-wide, and leaving either mutated would truncate or reshape every stack
the target itself takes, which is precisely the shim changing the behaviour of the thing it measures.
Verified: a `prepareStackTrace` the target installed is the one still installed after a capture. There is no
await between set and restore, so no other asynchronous context can observe the window.

## What this does not answer

**How many frames it takes to reach the repository's own file.** That is measurement 2 and it is the one
that decides whether any of this is useful. The cost of being wrong about it is now priced, which is the
whole contribution of this document: a bound too small to reach past the SDK returns the SDK's own frames,
and raising it to fifty is affordable.

**It was measured on synthetic stacks, not through a real SDK.** No model SDK is installed in this
repository or in the corpus caches, so the depth from `fetch` to a repository frame is unmeasured here. The
cost figures do not depend on that, because they are flat in depth; the usefulness question depends on it
entirely.

**Nothing about source maps.** Overriding `prepareStackTrace` during the capture means a target's own
source-map hook is not consulted for our frames, so a transpiled repository reports a location in its build
output. That is measurement 3 and this measurement makes it more urgent rather than less.

**No Python shim exists**, so the Python figures have no instrumented denominator to sit beside, only a bare
request. They are a cost for work that has not been designed.
